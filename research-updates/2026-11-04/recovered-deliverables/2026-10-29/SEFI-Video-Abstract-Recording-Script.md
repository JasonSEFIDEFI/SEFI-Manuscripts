**Delivery:** About three minutes at a calm speaking pace. Record each scene separately. Read only the quoted text; the headings and visual directions are for the edit.

**On screen:** You, framed chest-up, followed by the manuscript title. Keep SEFI Research Program as the primary identity.

**On screen:** Two colored arrows circulating in opposite directions on internal phase diagrams. Label these **phase orientation**, not physical particle trajectories.

> The central picture is a shared underlying field with internal phase structure. In a simple example, two components evolve with opposite phase directions, producing an orbit signature in the field's internal state. How those phase directions relate to physical temporal orientation remains an open question.

**On screen:** The action, field equation, and radial-profile illustration. Label the profile **numerical scalar-field benchmark**.

These calculations give the interpretation a precise mathematical realization, with stability questions stated explicitly.

## Scene 4 - Observation

**On screen:** A field configuration, a detector trajectory, and a highlighted sampling window. Show the detector response as a separate output.

> The field, the model, and the interpretation are distinct. Observation requires another ingredient: a defined detector response. We distinguish a persistent concentration of field energy from a detection event, and examine how an observer's trajectory and coupling enter the measurement description.

## Scene 5 - The photonics mapping

**On screen:** A two-path optical diagram and interference curves. Label the curves **analytic illustration**. Do not show simulated curves as experimental measurements.

> In photonics, the mapping connects complex mode amplitudes, relative phase, and optical readout. Changing a path phase changes the interference pattern. Established optics provides a benchmark for checking this mapping, while stronger claims about quantum error correction require a specified encoding, noise model, and recovery process.

## Scene 6 - The DNA mapping

**On screen:** An idealized double helix with radius and pitch marked, then the curvature and torsion formulas. Label the drawing **ideal helix geometry**.

> For DNA, the mapping is geometric. Radius and pitch determine an ideal helix's curvature and torsion. Those quantities connect a spatial phase pattern to explicit geometric invariants. Testing a proposed physical coupling requires measured structures and comparison with established molecular or elastic models.

## Scene 7 - The cosmology mapping

**On screen:** An expanding coordinate grid and the density curves from the manuscript. Distinguish **conserved model charge** from **field energy**.

> In cosmology, we examine the field on an expanding spacetime. We derive how the density associated with a conserved current dilutes with expansion, and distinguish that result from the evolution of energy, which also depends on pressure. A fully self-consistent gravitational solution remains a further task.

## Scene 8 - Purpose and next steps

**On screen:** Return to you, then fade to a closing title card after your final sentence. Hold the card independently of the camera clip.

> My aim is conceptual economy: making quantum geometry easier for a human reader to picture and reason about. These mappings organize that investigation. Electron and positron derivation remains open for future mathematics, vortex experiments, and field work. The goal is a framework we can understand, calculate with, and test.

## Recording checklist - do not read aloud

1. Record in **landscape, preferably 1080p**, with the camera at eye level and a steady chest-up framing. Leave a little space around your head for cropping. Use a plain, quiet background and light your face evenly.
2. If Camera records the video and Studio One records the microphone, start **both for the same take**. Say the scene and take number, clap once visibly, then wait three seconds before reading. The clap gives us an audio/video synchronization marker.
3. Read naturally. Pause briefly between sentences. You do not need to speak the equations or the full manuscript title; those will appear in the graphics.
4. After each scene, remain still and quiet for **five seconds**. Finish the final word completely, then let your mouth return naturally to a relaxed position. Keep recording during that pause.
5. For a mistake, pause for three seconds and repeat the **whole sentence**. Keep both recordings running. This leaves a clean editing point.
6. Use matching filenames, such as `SEFI_S01_T01.mp4` and `SEFI_S01_T01.wav`. Continue through `S08`. Keep multiple takes when useful; identify the preferred take in a note.
7. Record ten seconds of room tone at the end with the microphone running and nobody speaking.

## Editing plan

- Use your on-camera introduction and closing. Crop the background as needed after reviewing the footage.
- Cover the middle scenes with the manuscript's mathematical illustrations while retaining your synchronized microphone narration.
- Keep captions synchronized to complete sentences. Place transitions in pauses, never across a spoken word.
- Let the closing graphic carry the ending, rather than freezing on an unfinished facial expression.
- The three application mappings are mathematical correspondences. Keep the manuscript's illustration labels visible so they are not presented as experimental confirmation.

This script matches the current manuscript's terminology and scope. SEFI names the interpretation; the action and equations specify a particular model used to investigate it.
